## About Azerbaijan

Azerbaijan sits on the Caspian Sea between Russia and Iran. Most people are Muslim and speak a language close to Turkish. The food is close to Persian and Turkish cooking, with lots of rice, lamb, herbs, saffron, and flatbread. Azerbaijan sat on the old trade routes, so spices like saffron and fennel became part of everyday cooking.

## The Dish

Shorgoghal is a flaky, spiced pastry from Azerbaijan. It's baked for Novruz, the spring new year, when families make big batches of pastries to share. Thin layers of buttered dough are rolled up with a filling of fennel, cumin, and turmeric. Expect a flaky, buttery roll that's savory and a little earthy from the spices.

## Review

Delicious savory pastry. These were excellent with a sweet jam. The jam contrast really emphasized the spice. I screwed this recipe up a little, though. I didn't add enough butter or spice to the bread, so the spice didn't come through as much as it should have. I would definitely make these again, as they were a big hit.

## Shorgoghal

- Serves: 10 or more (about 20 rolls)
- Time: About 3 hours

## Ingredients and directions

### Ingredients

#### Dough

- 2¼ tsp active dry yeast (1 packet)
- 1 cup warm water (105–110°F)
- 6 cups all-purpose flour, plus extra for kneading
- 2 tsp salt
- 1 cup milk, warmed
- Generous pinch of saffron threads
- 1 large egg, room temperature
- 1½ sticks butter, melted and cooled

#### Filling

- 2 tbsp fennel seeds
- 2 tbsp cumin seeds (or anise seeds)
- ½ tsp turmeric
- 1 tsp salt
- ¼ tsp ground black pepper
- 2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1 stick butter, melted

#### Layering

- 3 sticks butter, clarified, for spreading

#### Topping

- 2 egg yolks + 1 tbsp water, for an egg wash
- Poppy seeds
- Flaky sea salt, for finishing (optional)

### Directions

1. Steep the saffron threads in the warm milk for 10 minutes until deep gold.
2. Dissolve the yeast in the warm water and let stand 5–10 minutes until foamy.
3. Mix the saffron milk, yeast mixture, and egg into the flour, salt, and butter. Knead until smooth and elastic.
4. Cover and let rise for about 1 hour, until doubled in size.
5. While the dough rises, toast the fennel and cumin seeds for 60–90 seconds until fragrant, then grind into a rough powder.
6. Melt 1 stick of butter and stir in the turmeric, salt, pepper, and ground spices.
7. Add just enough flour for the paste to turn cohesive and crumbly.
8. Divide the dough into 8–10 equal balls.
9. Roll each ball out as thin as possible, close to 1 mm thick. You should be able to see through it. Try to make each one roughly the same size and shape.
10. Use the largest piece as the base and spread the clarified butter over it. You're basically faking lamination in the dough.
11. Stack another rolled-out piece on top and coat it with butter. Repeat until all the pieces are stacked, but don't butter the top layer.
12. Cut the stack into strips about 1½–2 cm wide.
13. Roll each strip into a tight spiral like a cinnamon roll, tucking the end underneath, and move it to a parchment-lined baking sheet.
14. Pull the middle of each spiral open without tearing all the way through, so you have a small well. Add 1–2 tbsp of the spice filling to the well, then fold the dough over it and pinch closed. You'll end up with a roll whose spiraled top hides the filling underneath.
15. Preheat the oven to 350°F.
16. Flatten each roll gently and let rest 10–15 minutes.
17. Brush with the egg wash and top with poppy seeds.
18. Bake 40 minutes, until deep golden brown.
19. Finish with a light pinch of flaky sea salt right out of the oven. Let cool slightly before eating.

Note: Serve these with a sweet jam. Don't add too much flour to the filling, or the spices won't melt into the bread. Next time I'd make it more like a cinnamon roll, with the spices spread over the dough before rolling it. You'd get spice in every bite, and it would be distributed more evenly.
