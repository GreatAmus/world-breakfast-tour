## Our choice for Albania

Petulla are fried dough balls and a common Albanian breakfast, especially on weekends and holidays. Families make a big batch and everyone eats them warm. There are two ways to serve them: honey and powdered sugar, or jam and feta. Expect a light, puffy dough with a crisp outside, a lot like a donut hole without the sweetness. My version is a quick yogurt batter you spoon straight into hot oil, so there's no waiting for the dough to rise.

## At our table

Albania was a strong start. The fried dough came out just right: light and airy, with salty feta and sweet jam on top. That combination took it from simple fried dough to something we'd happily eat again. We all loved it, which gave us the motivation to keep this project going. No complaints at all.

## Breakfast context: Albania

Albania is a small country on the Balkan coast, right across the Adriatic Sea from Italy. It was part of the Ottoman Empire for over 400 years, so the food mixes Mediterranean and Turkish cooking. People eat a lot of olive oil, feta, yogurt, vegetables, and bread. Most of the country is mountains and farmland, so meals are built around what local farms produce, especially dairy.


## Recipe

### Ingredients

- 2 cups all-purpose flour
- 2 tsp baking powder
- ½ tsp salt
- 1 tsp sugar
- ¾ cup warm water
- ¼ cup plain yogurt
- 2 cups vegetable oil, for frying
- 2 tbsp powdered sugar, for serving
- 2 tbsp honey, for serving

### Directions

1. Whisk together the flour, baking powder, salt, and sugar in a bowl. Add the warm water and yogurt and stir until a thick, sticky batter forms. Drizzle in more water until it's the right consistency.
2. Pour the oil into a heavy skillet or saucepan to about 1 inch deep. Heat over medium-high until it reaches around 350°F.
3. Drop heaping spoonfuls of batter into the hot oil. Fry until deep golden on the bottom, about 2 minutes, then flip and fry another 90 seconds. They should puff up nicely.
4. Transfer to a paper towel-lined plate. Serve immediately, dusted with the powdered sugar and drizzled with the honey.

Note: Feta cheese and a little jam on the side is the traditional savory-sweet combo.
