## The dish

Akassa is a soft, slightly sour dough made from fermented cornmeal, a bit like polenta, eaten across Benin and Togo. Here it's paired with gbédjédjén, a spicy stew of beef and hot peppers. Our akassa used masa harina and Greek yogurt to imitate the fermented tang.

## What we thought

Surprisingly good for how simple it was. The spicy stew was a nice contrast to the tangy corn dough. We gave it a 3.7. It was good, but we wouldn't make it regularly.

## Ingredients and directions

### Ingredients

- 1 cup Maseca
- ½ cup plain Greek yogurt
- 2½ cups cold water
- A pinch of salt

### Directions

1. Whisk the Maseca with the Greek yogurt and 1 cup of the cold water until a smooth creamy paste if formed. Whisk until there are no lumps left. Leave this on the counter overnight.
2. Boil the remaining water in a pot with the salt.
3. Turn the heat down to medium-low and then pour the Maseca-yogurt mix into the boiling water while whisking constantly.
4. Use a wooden spoon to stir the mixture, cooking for about 12 minutes. The mixture will turn into a thick paste-like dough.
5. While the dough is hot, divide it into 3-4 portions. Use plastic wrap to form the dough into balls. Once its at room temperature, sever with the stew on top.

### Gbédjédjén

### Ingredients

- 1 lb ground beef
- 3 tbsp vegetable oil
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 can of chopped tomatoes
- 1 onion, diced
- 2 habaneros or red jalapenos (depending on the spice level you want)
- 5-6 basil leaves, chopped
- 1 beef bouillon cube
- 1 cup beef broth
- Salt and pepper to taste

### Directions

1. Brown the beef, pouring off extra oil. Move the beef to another dish.
2. Heat the oil in a pan. Once hot, add the onions and sauté for 2-3 minutes until they become translucent.
3. Add the tomato pace, chopped tomatoes, and basil.  Cook to reduce the sauce.
4. Reduce the heat to medium-low. Add the beef back to the pot along with the beef broth and bouillon. Simmer until the stew thickens up.
