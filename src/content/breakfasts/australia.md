## About Australia

Australia is a country and a whole continent. It started as a British colony, and later immigrants from Europe and Asia changed the food a lot. Australians eat a lot of beef, lamb, and seafood, and they love brunch cafés. Their food is laid back and simple, a lot like American food, with a British accent.

## The Dish

Pikelets are small, thick pancakes and a classic Australian treat for breakfast or afternoon tea. Australian kids grow up on them, and thanks to the show Bluey, a lot of American kids know them too. Compared to American pancakes, they're smaller, sweeter, and less fluffy. Expect a soft, sweet little pancake that's great with jam and whipped cream.

## Review

There might be more iconic Australian dishes, but you can't have a child under 10 and not make these. Everyone loves pancakes.

## Pikelets

- Serves: 4 (about 20 pikelets)
- Time: About 30 minutes

## Ingredients and directions

### Ingredients

#### Pikelets

- 1 egg
- 3 tbsp sugar
- 1 tsp golden syrup (or honey)
- 1 cup milk
- 1½ cups all-purpose flour
- 3 tsp baking powder
- ¼ tsp baking soda
- Pinch of salt
- 2 tsp butter, melted

#### To serve

- Butter
- Good-quality jam
- ½ cup heavy cream whipped to soft peaks with 2 tbsp powdered sugar (or a can of whipped cream)

### Directions

1. Mix the sugar, milk, egg, melted butter, and golden syrup together.
2. Mix the flour, baking powder, baking soda, and salt together, then add it to the egg mixture. Blend the two, but don't overmix.
3. Grease a pan with butter over low-medium heat.
4. Add about 2 tbsp of batter to the pan in a circle.
5. When bubbles appear on the surface of the batter, flip the pancake over. Cook until both sides are a light brown.
6. Serve with jam and whipped cream.
