## About Algeria

Algeria is the biggest country in Africa, sitting on the Mediterranean coast with most of its land in the Sahara. The people are mostly Arab and Berber, and France ruled the country for over 100 years. Wheat and semolina are the base of most meals, as couscous, bread, and flatbread. In a hot, dry country, grains store well and go a long way, so bread shows up at almost every meal.

## The Dish

Msemen is a square, flaky flatbread made with semolina and flour. You stretch the dough really thin, fold it into a square, and cook it on a griddle. It's a breakfast favorite across North Africa because it's made from pantry staples and goes with anything, sweet or savory. In Algeria it's usually eaten with honey and mint tea or coffee. Expect a crisp, flaky outside with chewy layers inside, a little like a paratha.

## Review

This was good as a base but needs a topping to make it delicious. It had a nice crunch on the outside, but it was plain on its own, and the honey was necessary. It would have been better if I'd cooked it in butter. Less healthy, though. Adding sharp cheddar cheese on top made it super good.


## Msemen (layered square flatbread)

- Serves: 4 (2 flatbreads each)
- Time: About 1½ hours

## Ingredients and directions

### Ingredients

- ¾ cup all-purpose flour
- ½ cup fine semolina
- ½ tsp sugar
- ½ tsp salt
- ¼ tsp anise seeds, lightly crushed
- ⅓ cup warm water
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1½ tbsp fine semolina, for folding
- 1½ tbsp good-quality honey

### Directions

1. Melt the butter and mix it with the olive oil in a small bowl. Set this aside for layering the dough.
2. Combine the flour, semolina, sugar, salt, and anise seeds. Gradually add the warm water and knead firmly for 8–10 minutes until smooth and slightly tacky. The dough should feel silkier than bread dough. It took me a bit more than ⅓ cup of water for the dough to come together.
3. Divide into 8 equal balls. Coat each ball in the butter-oil mixture and arrange them on a Silpat. Cover with a damp cloth and rest for 30 minutes.
4. Stretch and press the dough until it's as thin as you can possibly get it. Mine could let light through. Drizzle with the butter-oil mixture and dust lightly with the folding semolina. The semolina creates separation between the layers.
5. Fold the left third over the center, then the right third over that, like a letter. Then fold the top third down and the bottom third up to form a rough square. Press gently to flatten slightly. Repeat with all the balls.
6. Rest the folded squares for 10 minutes. Heat a pan over medium heat.
7. Smash the squares down using your palm or a rolling pin.
8. Cook in the pan for 3–4 minutes per side, pressing occasionally with a spatula, until deeply golden with dark patches and visibly flaky at the edges.
9. Serve immediately with honey.
