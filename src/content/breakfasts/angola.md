## About Angola

Angola is on the southwest coast of Africa. It was a Portuguese colony until 1975, so Portuguese is still the official language, and you can taste that history in the food. Most people eat a thick porridge called funge, made from cassava or corn, with fish, beans, and greens. Cassava and corn grow well in Angola's climate, so they became the base of everyday meals.

## The Dish

Filhós are light, crispy fried dough fritters. They came from Portugal and are made in a lot of Portuguese-influenced countries, often for holidays and celebrations. Angolan versions lean on local flavors like coconut and banana. Expect a crisp outside and a soft, airy inside, with warm spices and coconut. Mine were made with coconut milk and topped with powdered sugar and sliced banana.

## Review

These ones have a distinctly Central African flavor with the banana and coconut.

## Filhós

- Serves: 4 (about 20 fritters)
- Time: About 1 hour

## Ingredients and directions

### Ingredients

#### Fritters

- 2 cups flour
- 1 tsp instant yeast
- 1 tbsp brown sugar
- ½ tsp salt
- 1 tsp cinnamon
- ¼ tsp nutmeg
- 1 tsp vanilla
- ¾ cup coconut milk
- 1 egg
- 2 cups vegetable oil, for frying

#### To serve

- 2 tbsp powdered sugar
- 1 ripe banana, sliced

### Directions

1. Mix the flour, yeast, brown sugar, salt, cinnamon, and nutmeg in a large bowl.
2. Add the coconut milk, vanilla, and egg. Mix until blended.
3. Cover the batter and rest for 30 minutes. The batter will be puffed up but extremely sticky.
4. Heat the oil to about 350°F in a frying pan. Test the heat with a small drop of batter. It should sizzle immediately and float to the surface.
5. Drop heaped tablespoons of batter into the oil in batches. Don't overcrowd. Fry 2–3 minutes per side until deep golden brown, turning once.
6. Drain on paper towels for 1 minute, then dust generously with powdered sugar. Add the bananas on top.
