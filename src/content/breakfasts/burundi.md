## About Burundi

Burundi is a small, landlocked country in East Africa, on the shore of Lake Tanganyika. It's one of the most crowded countries in Africa, and most people farm small plots. It has a rich food culture built around locally grown staples such as beans, bananas, cassava, and sweet potatoes. Beans are a big source of protein, since meat is expensive.

## The Dish

Ibiharage n'ibitoke is a traditional Burundian dish combining beans (ibiharage) and plantains (ibitoke). Beans are eaten almost every day in Burundi, and pairing them with plantains makes a cheap, filling meal. Expect a savory, hearty bean stew with tomato and ginger, and soft plantains on the side.

## Review

How delicious this dish was came as a huge surprise for me, as I typically do not like African food. The beans and plantains were really good together. I shouldn't have been surprised, because the mix of sweet and salty usually ends up rating pretty high. This dish was also a massive hit with my daughter, who went back for seconds. She gave it a 4.5/5, which I think makes this her second favorite dish! Personally, I thought the dish was more middle of the road than the rest of the family did, but my one lower rating was trumped by the three higher ratings. For me, the negative was the texture. The flavor was good if you ate all of the components together, but the texture was mushy.


## Ibiharage n’ibitoke (beans and plantains)

- Serves: 4 (about 1½ cups each)
- Time: About 45 minutes

## Ingredients and directions

### Ingredients

- 2 plantains, peeled and cut into 1-inch chunks
- 1 tbsp oil, plus 1 tsp if using yellow plantains
- Salt, to taste
- 1 large onion, finely diced
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1½ tsp freshly grated ginger
- 1 medium tomato, finely chopped
- 2 tsp tomato paste
- 1 can (15 oz) kidney beans, drained and rinsed
- 1 can (15 oz) navy beans, drained and rinsed
- ¾ cup water or vegetable broth, plus more as needed
- ½ tsp black pepper
- ¼ tsp ground coriander
- ¼ tsp paprika
- Pinch of chili flakes (optional)
- 1 small bay leaf
- ¼ cup chopped cilantro
- 2 tsp lemon juice

### Directions

1. If the plantains are green, simmer the chunks in a pot of lightly salted water for 12–18 minutes, until fork-tender, then drain. If the plantains are yellow, heat 1 tsp of oil in a pan, just enough to coat the bottom, and cook the plantains with a pinch of salt over low heat until a slightly crispy brown coating forms.
2. Heat 1 tbsp of oil in a heavy pot over medium heat. Add the onion and cook for 8–10 minutes until soft and lightly golden.
3. Stir in the garlic and ginger and cook for about 1 minute. Add the pepper, coriander, paprika, and chili flakes.
4. Add the chopped tomato and tomato paste. Cook for 5–7 minutes, stirring occasionally.
5. Stir in the kidney beans, navy beans, bay leaf, and water or broth, and bring to a gentle simmer.
6. Cook uncovered for 10–15 minutes, stirring occasionally. Mash some of the navy beans against the side of the pot and stir them into the sauce. Add small amounts of water if needed to keep the beans moist. Adjust the seasoning to make it pop. I used probably 1 tsp of salt and added slightly more coriander and paprika.
7. Remove the bay leaf and stir in the cilantro and lemon juice.
8. Spoon the beans into bowls and arrange the warm plantains alongside or on top.
