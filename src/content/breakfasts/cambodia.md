## About Cambodia

Cambodia is in Southeast Asia, between Thailand and Vietnam, and it's home to the temples of Angkor Wat. Most Cambodians are Khmer and Buddhist. The food is built around rice and fish, with fresh herbs, pickled vegetables, and sauces that balance sour, salty, and sweet. The Mekong River and Tonlé Sap lake supply huge amounts of fish, and the wet climate is perfect for rice.

## The Dish

Bai sach chrouk translates roughly to "pork and rice" in Khmer. This dish is one of Cambodia's most beloved traditional breakfasts. Thin slices of pork are marinated with garlic, sugar, soy sauce, and fish sauce, then grilled until fragrant and caramelized. Street vendors grill it over charcoal early in the morning, which is why it's such a common way to start the day. The pork is served over rice, often with pickled vegetables, fresh cucumber, and a light bowl of broth. Expect sweet, smoky, slightly salty meat with crunchy, tangy pickles. I added a fried egg on top.

## Review

I liked how easy this dish was to make and how fresh it felt, with several different vegetables. It feels like a complete meal, unlike many other breakfasts that are heavy on bread. However, the dish itself is bland compared to other BBQ meat recipes, and the flavors don't stand out. If I were going to revise this recipe further, I'd increase the salt in the marinade and pickle the vegetables overnight to get a real sour and sweet flavor. The dish was good but needed an additional flavor kick to make it great. The lack of a distinct "WOW" factor made it something I wouldn't cook often. The family was consistent on this one.


## Bai sach chrouk (grilled pork and rice)

- Serves: 6 (about 1 cup of rice and ⅓ lb of pork each)
- Time: About 1 hour, plus marinating the pork the night before

## Ingredients and directions

### Grilled Pork

### Ingredients

#### Pork and marinade

- 2 lb boneless pork shoulder
- ⅓ cup coconut milk
- 2½ tbsp fish sauce
- 1½ tbsp regular soy sauce
- 2½ tbsp brown sugar
- 2 tbsp minced garlic
- 1 tsp black pepper
- 1 tbsp neutral oil, such as vegetable or canola
- 1 tbsp lime juice
- Oil for the grill or pan

#### Finishing glaze

- 2 tbsp brown sugar
- 2 tsp fish sauce
- ¼ cup water
- 2 tsp minced garlic

> **Know before you start:** The pork marinates overnight, so start it the night before.

### Directions

1. The night before, slice the pork against the grain into ¼-inch-thick pieces.
2. In a bowl, stir the coconut milk, fish sauce, soy sauce, brown sugar, garlic, pepper, oil, and lime juice together until the sugar dissolves.
3. Add the pork and press it into the marinade. Massage the meat briefly to work the marinade in. Cover and refrigerate overnight.
4. Combine the glaze ingredients in a small saucepan and bring to a simmer over medium-low heat. Simmer gently for about 5 minutes, until the sugar dissolves and the mixture becomes a light syrup. It should be thin enough to brush on the meat but thick enough to stick during cooking.
5. Take the pork out of the refrigerator at least 20 minutes before cooking. Remove the meat from the marinade and let the excess drip off. Clumps of sugar or garlic left on the meat will burn.
6. Heat a grill or cast iron pan until very hot.
7. Grill the pork in small batches until fully cooked. Timing depends on thickness. Very thin slices, like Korean BBQ cuts, take 1–2 minutes. Thicker slices may need up to 5 minutes per side. The finished meat should have dark golden-brown patches on both sides and be cooked through.
8. Brush both sides of the meat with the glaze, then return the pork to the grill for about 30 seconds per side to caramelize.

### Pickled Vegetables

### Ingredients

- 1 medium carrot, cut into matchsticks
- ½ small daikon radish, cut into matchsticks
- 1 small cucumber, cut into matchsticks
- ½ cup rice vinegar
- ¼ cup water
- 2 tbsp granulated sugar
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 tsp minced garlic
- 1 small Thai chili, thinly sliced (optional)

> **Know before you start:** These taste best made the night before.

### Directions

1. Combine the vinegar, water, sugar, and salt in a small saucepan. Heat over low heat until the sugar and salt dissolve.
2. Once the liquid has cooled, add the carrot, daikon, cucumber, garlic, and chili. Stir until all the vegetables are submerged.
3. Refrigerate for at least 1 hour, preferably overnight.

### Rice

### Ingredients

- 2 cups jasmine rice
- 3½ cups chicken stock
- Pinch of salt

### Directions

1. Rinse the rice until the water runs mostly clear.
2. Add the rice, stock, and salt to a pot.
3. Bring to a boil, then cover and reduce the heat to very low.
4. Cook for 20 minutes, covered, then fluff with a fork.

### Broth

### Ingredients

- 3 cups low-sodium chicken stock
- 1 tsp minced garlic
- 2 green onions, sliced
- 1 tsp fish sauce, plus more to taste
- ½ tsp black pepper
- Salt, to taste

### Directions

1. Combine all the broth ingredients in a saucepan and bring to a boil. Reduce the heat and simmer for 10 minutes.

### To Serve

### Ingredients

- Fresh cucumber slices
- Fresh cilantro
- Lime wedges
- 1 fried egg per person

### Directions

1. Spoon a mound of rice onto each plate.
2. Arrange the grilled pork over the rice.
3. Top with a pile of pickled vegetables and fresh cucumber.
4. Garnish with cilantro and lime.
5. Fry an egg for each plate and set it on top of the pork and pickled vegetables. The egg makes the dish feel like breakfast.
6. Serve with the hot broth in a small bowl on the side. Dip each bite in the broth as you eat.
