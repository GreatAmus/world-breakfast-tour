## Our choice for Brazil

Pão de queijo is a cheese bread made with tapioca flour, so it's naturally gluten-free. It comes from the state of Minas Gerais, and it's eaten for breakfast or as a snack with coffee all over Brazil. Anyone who's ever eaten at a Brazilian restaurant has had it. Expect small, crisp-outside rolls that are soft, stretchy, and chewy inside, with a lot of cheese flavor.

## At our table

They're easy to make and always a hit. They come out deliciously chewy. I don't love them myself, but they're my wife's absolute favorite. She gives this one a 5/5. I'd say 4/5, mostly because pão de queijo isn't very interesting to make.

## Breakfast context: Brazil

Brazil is the biggest country in South America and the only one that speaks Portuguese. It has huge Indigenous, African, and European communities, and the food mixes all three. Rice and beans are everyday food, along with beef, cassava, and tropical fruit. Cassava is native to Brazil, so cassava flour and tapioca show up everywhere.


## Recipe

### Ingredients

- 3 cups tapioca flour
- 1 cup whole milk
- ½ cup water
- ¼ cup vegetable oil
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter
- 1½ tsp kosher salt
- 2 large eggs
- 1 cup shredded mozzarella
- ½ cup shredded sharp cheddar
- ½ cup grated parmesan

### Directions

1. In a saucepan, combine the milk, water, oil, butter, and salt. Bring to a gentle rolling boil over medium-high heat.
2. Place the tapioca flour in a large heat-safe bowl. Pour the boiling liquid over the flour and mix immediately with a wooden spoon or a stand mixer on low. The dough will look like lumpy wallpaper paste.
3. Let the mixture cool for 5–10 minutes, until just warm to the touch.
4. Add the eggs one at a time, mixing after each egg. The dough will become glossy, silky, and stretchy.
5. Add all the cheese and mix until combined.
6. Cover and rest in the fridge for 30 minutes.
7. Preheat the oven to 375°F.
8. With wet hands (to prevent sticking), roll the dough into golf-ball-sized spheres. Space them 1 inch apart on a parchment-lined baking sheet.
9. Bake for 20 minutes, until they puff up dramatically and develop golden cheese freckles on top.

Note: Don't use pre-shredded cheese. It's coated in potato starch to prevent sticking, which ruins the texture.
