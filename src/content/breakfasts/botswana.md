## About Botswana

Botswana is a landlocked country in southern Africa, home to the Kalahari Desert. Cattle are a huge part of life there. Most meals are beef with a thick porridge made from sorghum or corn. Sorghum handles drought better than most grains, so it grows where other crops won't.

## The Dish

Bogobe is a thick porridge made from sorghum or cornmeal, and it's the basic everyday starch in Botswana. For breakfast it's often served softer, with eggs and a tomato and onion relish. Expect a thick, mild porridge with a runny egg and a sweet, savory tomato relish on top. I had to substitute several ingredients, since authentic sorghum meal and lerotse are hard to find.

## Review

This dish was just bland. Porridges are not my favorite, and this did not change my mind. I usually really like butternut squash, but it did not complement the masa harina. Similarly, the sourness of the Greek yogurt did not really mix with the sweetness of the butternut squash. The flavors of the relish never mixed either, and it tasted like chopped onion and tomatoes dumped on eggs. The kids did not like it either. Without the egg, it's basically just a wet corn mix covered with squash. No one thought it was good, so we tossed it and ate something else.


## Bogobe (porridge with egg and tomato relish)

- Serves: 4 (about 1½ cups of porridge and 1 egg each)
- Time: About 1 hour

## Ingredients and directions

### Ingredients

#### Porridge

- 1 medium butternut squash, peeled, seeded, and cubed small
- 1½ cups masa harina, divided
- 3 cups water, plus 1 cup cold water
- ½ cup plain Greek yogurt
- 2 tbsp butter
- Salt, to taste

#### Eggs and relish

- 4 large eggs
- 1 medium onion, finely chopped
- 2 ripe tomatoes, finely chopped
- 1 garlic clove, minced
- ½ tsp curry powder
- 1 tbsp oil, plus more for frying the eggs
- Salt and black pepper, to taste

### Directions

1. Place the squash cubes in a large pot with 3 cups of water. Bring to a boil, cover, and simmer for 20 minutes, until fork-tender.
2. Mash the squash in its cooking water with a whisk, potato masher, or immersion blender until you have a smooth, boiling orange puree.
3. In a small bowl, mix 1 cup of the masa harina with 1 cup of cold water into a smooth, runny paste. Pour it slowly into the boiling puree, whisking vigorously.
4. Reduce the heat to low. Gradually stir in the remaining ½ cup of masa harina with a sturdy wooden spoon.
5. Cover and steam on low for 12 minutes, stirring once halfway so the bottom doesn't burn.
6. Meanwhile, heat 1 tbsp of oil in a skillet over medium heat. Add the onion and garlic and cook for 3 minutes, until soft and fragrant.
7. Stir in the curry powder, then add the tomatoes with a pinch of salt and pepper. Cook 4–5 minutes, crushing the tomatoes with your spoon, until thick and jammy. Transfer to a small dish and wipe the skillet clean.
8. Add a splash of oil to the skillet and fry the eggs sunny-side up, keeping the yolks runny. Season with salt and pepper.
9. Turn off the heat under the porridge. Stir in the butter and Greek yogurt. Season with salt to taste.
10. Spoon a generous mound of porridge into each bowl and top with the warm relish and an egg. Pierce the yolk so it runs into the relish and porridge.

Note: Traditional bogobe uses sorghum meal, which can be hard to find. I used masa harina instead, with butternut squash and Greek yogurt.
