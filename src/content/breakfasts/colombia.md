## About Colombia

Colombia is in the northwest corner of South America, with coasts on both the Caribbean and the Pacific and the Andes running through the middle. Most Colombians have a mix of Spanish, Indigenous, and African roots. People eat a lot of rice, beans, corn, plantains, and meat, and arepas show up at almost every meal. Corn and plantains grow all over the country's warm valleys, and the mountain farm country around Medellín built its food around big, filling plates that last through a long workday.

## The Dish

Calentado means "reheated." It started as a way to use up last night's rice and beans by frying them together for breakfast, and the paisa version comes from the Antioquia region around Medellín. It's a full plate, usually served with a fried egg, an arepa, sausage, sweet plantains, and avocado. Expect savory rice and beans with a runny egg on top, a crispy corn arepa with melted cheese inside, and sweet, caramelized plantains on the side. I used canned kidney beans and beef instead of dried beans and pork, and linguiça instead of Colombian chorizo.

## Review

I had high hopes for Colombia, but the flavors fell flat. I think that was more my fault than the cuisine's. I stumbled through putting this recipe together, and it would have been better, and a lot easier, if I'd kept the beans simple and stirred refried beans into the hogao. There were a lot of moving parts, and it ran past my goal of 2 hours of cooking, not counting wait times. None of it was hard, but it all took time.

Nothing really came together. Mixing the beans with rice and hogao didn't make them any better, the ají didn't add much flavor, and the arepas weren't great. My wife said they tasted like real arepas, but the crunchy outside and soft corn inside didn't do it for me. Everyone agreed on the score. It was fine, but it's definitely not making our regular rotation.

## Calentado paisa (rice and beans breakfast plate)

- Serves: 4 (about 1 cup of calentado, 1 arepa, 1 egg, and a quarter of the linguiça each)
- Time: About 1¼ hours, plus about 2¼ hours of cooking the night before

## Ingredients and directions

> **Know before you start:** Make the hogao, beans, and rice the night before, since the calentado needs cold rice and beans. In the morning, work in this order: make the ají and arepa dough, cook the arepas, make the calentado and plantains, brown the linguiça, then fry the eggs last. Two skillets and a griddle make it easier.

### Hogao (Tomato and Scallion Sauce)

### Ingredients

- 3 tbsp neutral oil
- 1 cup thinly sliced scallions, white and light green parts
- ½ cup finely diced yellow onion
- 1½ lb ripe Roma tomatoes, finely chopped (about 3½ cups)
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- ¼ tsp ground cumin
- ½ tsp fine salt
- ¼ tsp black pepper

### Directions (make the night before)

1. Heat the oil in a saucepan over medium-low heat. Add the scallions and onion and cook 6–8 minutes, stirring, until soft but not browned.
2. Add the garlic and cumin and stir for 30 seconds.
3. Add the tomatoes, salt, and pepper. Cook uncovered for 20–30 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the tomatoes collapse into a soft, spoonable sauce. Stop before it turns into a dry paste.
4. Set aside ½ cup for the beans. Refrigerate the rest.

### Beef and Beans

### Ingredients

- 8 oz flat steak, cut into bite-sized pieces
- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 4 cups water
- ½ cup hogao, divided
- 2 cans (15 oz) red kidney beans, drained and rinsed
- ½ green plantain, peeled and cut into ¼-inch pieces
- ½ cup finely grated carrot
- ½ tsp fine salt

### Directions (make the night before)

1. Heat the oil in a pot over medium-high heat. Brown the beef on all sides, about 5 minutes.
2. Add the water and ¼ cup of the hogao. Bring to a boil, then lower to a gentle simmer. Partially cover and cook 1 hour.
3. Add the beans, green plantain, carrot, salt, and the remaining ¼ cup of hogao. Simmer uncovered for 30 more minutes, until the beef is tender, the plantain is soft, and the broth lightly coats a spoon.
4. Mash a few spoonfuls of the beans and plantain against the side of the pot and stir them back in.
5. Refrigerate overnight.

Note: Next time I'd keep the beans simple and stir refried beans into the hogao instead.

### Rice

### Ingredients

- 1 cup white rice

### Directions (make the night before)

1. Cook the rice according to the package directions. Measure out 2 cups for the calentado.
2. Refrigerate overnight.

### Ají (Cilantro Hot Sauce)

### Ingredients

- ⅓ cup finely chopped cilantro, including tender stems
- ¼ cup finely chopped scallion greens
- 1 tbsp finely minced jalapeño
- 1 tbsp lime juice
- 1 tbsp white vinegar
- 2 tbsp water
- ⅛ tsp fine salt

### Directions

1. Mix everything together and let it sit 15 minutes before serving.

### Cheese-Stuffed Arepas

### Ingredients

- 1 cup white masarepa
- 1 cup warm water
- ¼ tsp fine salt
- ½ tbsp unsalted butter, melted
- ¾ cup shredded low-moisture mozzarella
- 2 tbsp crumbled queso fresco
- 2 tsp neutral oil

### Directions

1. Heat the oven to 350°F and set a wire rack over a baking sheet.
2. Stir the salt into the warm water, then add the melted butter. Stir in the masarepa, knead briefly, and let the dough rest 10 minutes.
3. Mix the mozzarella and queso fresco.
4. Divide the dough into 4 balls. Press a deep, wide well into each ball, pack in a quarter of the cheese, and pinch the dough closed over it.
5. Flatten each ball into a disc about 4 inches wide and ½ inch thick. Patch any cracks with damp fingers.
6. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium heat. Cook the arepas 5–6 minutes per side, until golden with a few dark spots.
7. Move them to the rack and bake 10–15 minutes, until the cheese is melted.

### Calentado

### Ingredients

- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- ½ cup hogao
- 2 cups beans with some of the beef, scooped out with a slotted spoon
- ¼ cup bean broth
- 2 cups cold cooked rice

### Directions

1. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Add the hogao, beans, and bean broth and simmer 2–3 minutes.
2. Fold in the rice, breaking up any clumps. Cook 6–10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until hot all the way through.
3. Spread the mixture flat and leave it alone for 2–3 minutes, until golden patches form on the bottom. Turn it over in large sections and keep warm over low heat.

### Brown Butter Plantains

### Ingredients

- 2 very ripe plantains (yellow with lots of black spots)
- ¼ cup neutral oil
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter
- ¼ tsp lime zest
- Pinch of salt

### Directions

1. Peel the plantains and slice them diagonally about ½ inch thick.
2. Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Fry the plantains in a single layer, 3–4 minutes per side, until deep golden and soft inside. Move them to a plate, pour off the oil, and wipe out the skillet.
3. Melt the butter over medium-low heat and swirl until it turns golden brown and smells nutty. Pour it into a heatproof bowl right away so it doesn't burn.
4. Right before serving, spoon the brown butter over the plantains and sprinkle with the salt and lime zest.

### Linguiça

### Ingredients

- 1 linguiça sausage (about 12 oz)
- 1 tsp neutral oil

### Directions

1. Cut the linguiça into ½-inch rounds.
2. Heat the oil in a skillet over medium heat. Brown the rounds 2–3 minutes per side, until hot all the way through.

### Putting It All Together

### Ingredients

- 4 large eggs
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 1 ripe avocado, sliced
- Pinch of salt
- Remaining hogao, warmed

### Directions

1. Heat 1 tbsp of the oil in a nonstick skillet over medium-high heat. Fry 2 eggs until the whites are set and the edges are crisp. Repeat with the rest of the oil and eggs.
2. Divide the calentado among 4 plates with the golden side up. Top each with an egg.
3. Top each egg with avocado slices, a pinch of salt, 1–2 tbsp of warm hogao, and 1–2 tsp of ají.
4. Add an arepa, the linguiça rounds, and a quarter of the plantains around the calentado.
