## About Antigua and Barbuda

Antigua and Barbuda is a two-island country in the eastern Caribbean. Most people there are descended from enslaved Africans brought to work the sugar plantations, and Britain ruled the islands until 1981. The food mixes African, British, and Caribbean cooking, with lots of seafood and tropical fruit. Fruit like bananas and mangoes grows easily in the warm climate, so it ends up in everything from breakfast to dessert.

## The Dish

Banana fritters are a common Caribbean breakfast and snack. They're popular because bananas are everywhere, and fritters are a great way to use up really ripe ones. Mashed bananas get mixed with flour and warm spices, then fried until crisp. Expect something like a small, crisp-edged banana pancake, sweet and soft in the middle. I served mine with a coconut lime dipping sauce.

## Review

The flavor was excellent, but I think they needed a bit more flour and baking powder, though. I adjusted the recipe based on what I would do if I made them again.

## Banana fritters with coconut-lime sauce

- Serves: 4 (about 16 fritters)
- Time: About 30 minutes

## Ingredients and directions

### Ingredients

#### Fritters

- 3 very ripe bananas (the blacker the better)
- 1 cup all-purpose flour
- 2 tbsp brown sugar
- 1½ tsp baking powder
- ½ tsp salt
- 1½ tsp ground cinnamon
- ½ tsp ground allspice
- ½ tsp ground nutmeg
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- 1 lime, zested and juiced
- 1 egg
- 3 tbsp coconut milk
- 2 tbsp coconut oil or vegetable oil, for frying

#### Coconut lime sauce

- ½ cup full-fat coconut milk
- 1 tbsp honey
- 1 tsp lime juice
- ¼ tsp salt

### Directions

1. Make the coconut lime sauce by whisking together the coconut milk, honey, lime juice, and salt in a small saucepan. Cook over low heat for 3–4 minutes. Set aside to let it thicken.
2. Mash the bananas in a bowl. Add the vanilla, lime juice and zest, egg, and coconut milk.
3. Add the flour, brown sugar, baking powder, salt, cinnamon, allspice, and nutmeg to the banana mixture. Fold until just combined. It should be the consistency of a thick pancake batter.
4. Heat the oil in a pan over medium heat until hot. Drop a tsp of batter in the pan to test the heat. If the oil is hot enough, it'll fry fast.
5. Drop about a tbsp of batter into the pan and cook until brown. It depends on the heat, but this usually takes 1–3 minutes on the first side. Flip and cook the other side until fully cooked. The second side always takes less time. Adjust the heat if it cooks too fast so the middle isn't undercooked. You want nice brown coloring with a crisp outside.
6. Serve with the coconut lime sauce.

Note: You could use coconut cream instead of coconut milk for a richer and thicker sauce. Coconut cream is also a great substitute in the batter, provided you reduce the flour slightly to keep the consistency of pancake batter.
